# Executive Checklists — Template

**Version:** 1.1.0 | **Licence:** CC BY 4.0

Copyable checklists for decisions, reviews and operation. Use them as prompts, not as a substitute for judgement.

## Execution and portfolio

Used with the [Executive Playbook](../frameworks/strategy-execution-governance-playbook.md) and [Digital Portfolio Governance](../frameworks/digital-portfolio-governance.md).

### Before an investment decision

- [ ] Problem, user and strategic outcome are explicit.
- [ ] Baseline, target, date and benefit owner are stated.
- [ ] Viable options include process change, reuse and defer / do nothing.
- [ ] Mandatory status, evidence and deadline are verified.
- [ ] Scoring evidence, confidence, lifecycle cost and capacity are visible.
- [ ] Dependencies, material risks and assurance needs have owners.
- [ ] Decision authority, conditions and review trigger are clear.

### Before delivery authorisation

- [ ] Scope boundaries, acceptance evidence and tolerances are approved.
- [ ] Executive sponsor, Initiative lead, Service owner and Benefit owner are named.
- [ ] Integrated plan, funding, skills and dependencies are credible.
- [ ] Security, privacy, architecture, quality and operational readiness are addressed proportionately.
- [ ] Adoption, communications, support and benefits measurement are planned.

### At each governance review

- [ ] Actuals are separated from forecasts and assumptions.
- [ ] Top risks and dependencies have owners and next actions.
- [ ] Tolerance breaches are escalated to the right authority.
- [ ] Decisions have options, a recommendation, an owner and a due date.
- [ ] Capacity and portfolio trade-offs are considered.
- [ ] Adoption and outcome evidence informs the next action.

### Portfolio health

- [ ] Every initiative has an Executive sponsor and Initiative lead.
- [ ] Mandatory commitments are visible.
- [ ] Prioritisation decisions and trade-offs are recorded.
- [ ] Strategic dependencies have owners and decided responses.
- [ ] Benefits have owners, baselines and measurement plans.
- [ ] The portfolio is periodically rebalanced and initiatives can be stopped or reshaped when evidence changes.

## Open source

Used with [Open Source Strategy & Governance](../frameworks/open-source-strategy-governance.md) and the [Open-source assessment](open-source-assessment.md).

### Adoption

- [ ] Need, users, outcome and alternatives are explicit.
- [ ] Project, source, version, dependencies and maintenance path are understood.
- [ ] Strategic-dependency status and governance health are assessed.
- [ ] Licence and intended-use questions have qualified review.
- [ ] Security, privacy, architecture and operations have proportionate assessment.
- [ ] Lifecycle cost includes internal skills, integration, support, updates and exit.
- [ ] Owners, acceptable use, exception authority and review date are clear.

### Contribution or stewardship

- [ ] Strategic rationale and expected value are stated.
- [ ] Participation mode, scope and mandate are explicit.
- [ ] Rights, security, legal and communications reviews are complete.
- [ ] Capacity, funding and continuity are realistic.
- [ ] Success measures and pause or exit conditions are agreed.

### Critical dependencies

- [ ] Maintainer and governance concentration are understood.
- [ ] Continuity options (mirror, fork, replace) are credible.
- [ ] The organisation knows whether it intends to remain a user, contribute, take part in stewardship or eventually replace the dependency.
- [ ] Exceptions are time-bounded and reviewed; actual costs and service outcomes are visible.

## Enterprise AI and RAG

Used with the [Enterprise AI & RAG Governance Blueprint](../frameworks/enterprise-ai-rag-governance-blueprint.md) and the [AI/RAG use-case assessment](ai-rag-use-case-assessment.md).

- [ ] The use case has a defined user, task and measurable outcome.
- [ ] RAG has been compared with simpler alternatives, including conventional search.
- [ ] Source owners, permissions and lifecycle rules are known.
- [ ] Access controls are enforced before retrieved content reaches the model.
- [ ] Citations and provenance are available and testable.
- [ ] Prompt injection and data disclosure risks are assessed.
- [ ] The evaluation set includes unanswerable and adversarial questions.
- [ ] Human oversight and escalation are defined.
- [ ] Strategic dependencies and exit implications are understood.
- [ ] Operating ownership, monitoring and incident response are funded.
- [ ] Benefits, quality and cost will be reviewed after deployment.

## Digital infrastructure and sustainability

Used with [Digital Sustainability and Data Centre Energy Governance](../frameworks/digital-sustainability-data-centre-governance.md) and the [Infrastructure capacity decision](infrastructure-capacity-decision.md).

### Before approving an initiative

- [ ] The service need, user outcome and demand forecast are evidenced.
- [ ] Optimisation, reuse, shared capacity and phased options were considered.
- [ ] Energy, grid, water, carbon, site and equipment impacts are visible.
- [ ] Resilience, security, service quality and local constraints are assessed.
- [ ] Lifecycle cost and key assumptions are explicit.
- [ ] Data boundaries, baseline, measure owner and review date are agreed.
- [ ] Delivery maturity, ownership, dependencies and exit approach are credible.

### During delivery and operation

- [ ] Actual load and forecast are reviewed against the approved mandate.
- [ ] Indicators use stable, documented boundaries.
- [ ] Supplier evidence is available and reviewable.
- [ ] Material variances trigger an accountable decision.
- [ ] Benefits are assessed using measured service output and resource use.
- [ ] Unused capacity, equipment and data are periodically reviewed.
